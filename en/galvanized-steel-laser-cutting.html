What is galvanising, and what should you know before cutting?

Galvanising coats steel with zinc that protects in two ways: it separates the metal from air and moisture, and it also protects electrochemically — the zinc corrodes instead of the steel if the surface is lightly scratched. That is why galvanised sheet lasts years where plain steel rusts within weeks.

The fact to know before cutting: the cut line itself is where the zinc coating is interrupted. The layer stays on both faces, but the edge becomes exposed steel across the sheet thickness. In most interior or sheltered uses that is not a practical problem, but for outdoor use with direct water exposure it is better to treat the edge with a zinc-rich paint.

We tell you this up front rather than after delivery, because small details like this decide whether a part lasts five years or gets replaced after one — and that is not something you should have to learn the hard way.

When to choose galvanised

  • When you need moisture resistance on a smaller budget than stainless, and the part’s appearance is not critical.
  • In HVAC and ducting work, where galvanised sheet is the actual market standard in Saudi Arabia.
  • For partially sheltered outdoor parts: canopies, equipment covers, electrical boxes and roofing.
  • Avoid it if the part will be heavily welded or given a premium finish coat — welding heat burns off zinc and needs extra treatment.
