What is laser engraving on metal?

Laser engraving focuses a laser beam onto the metal surface and changes it instantly: either by removing a very thin layer so the mark is recessed and can be felt by hand, or by heating the surface so its colour turns black, grey or white without removing any material. Either way the result is not ink on top of the metal — the metal itself has changed.

That is the essential difference between laser engraving and printing, stickers or chemical etching: a sticker peels, print scratches off with solvents and sun, but a laser mark lasts as long as the metal — resisting heat, oils, cleaning agents, industrial washing and UV.

In the Saudi market several names are used for roughly the same service: laser engraving, laser marking, laser writing and fibre marking. They all point to the same thing — the difference is in the depth of the mark, not in the technology.

Engraving, marking or etching? One line each

  • Surface marking: no material removed, only the surface colour changes — the fastest and most economical, ideal for text, logos and codes on visible parts.
  • Engraving: a layer measured in fractions of a millimetre is removed so the mark is recessed and tangible — preferred when the part must stay readable after years of wear.
  • Deep engraving: several passes to reach a greater depth — for moulds and spare parts exposed to abrasion or later coating.
  • Black marking: special settings that give a deep, high-contrast black on stainless without scratching the surface — the best-looking option for premium products and gifts.

You do not need to know which type suits you — send us a photo of the part and tell us where it will be used, and we recommend the right type and settings before quoting.

There is no difference in the service — “fibre marking” (Fiber Laser Marking) is the technical name of the machine type, a fibre laser dedicated to metals, while “laser engraving” and “laser marking” are the everyday names for the same work in the Saudi market. Everything described here is produced on a fibre laser. The only real difference is the depth of the mark: surface marking, recessed engraving, or deep engraving.
