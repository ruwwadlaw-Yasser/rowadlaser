What is the difference between copper and brass?

Copper is a pure metal with a reddish-orange colour, one of the best conductors of electricity and heat. Over time it develops a patina — dark or greenish — which some see as a defect and others treat as intended beauty in a design.

Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc with a warmer golden tone. It is harder than pure copper, easier to machine and polish, and holds its appearance longer before dulling — which is why it is preferred for plaques, interior décor, handles and logos.

Both share one challenge for a laser: high reflectivity. Modern fibre lasers are what made cutting them possible, safe and high quality — yet dialling in settings for each thickness is not something to rush; hurrying it means a rough edge or a drifting path.

Before you order copper cutting

  • Decide whether you want to keep the shine or allow a patina to form — that determines whether you need a clear protective lacquer after cutting.
  • Copper is relatively expensive, so we nest parts on the sheet more carefully to keep waste to a minimum.
  • Thin sheet suits décor and logos, while electrical applications are dictated by the cross-section needed to carry current.
  • Send a photo of similar work you like — agreeing colour and finish in advance prevents disputes after production.
