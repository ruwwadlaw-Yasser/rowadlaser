What makes a kitchen fitting succeed or fail

Restaurant owners buy fittings by size and price, then discover within months that the details were the problem: a sharp internal corner where residue collects and no sponge reaches, a perfectly flat top where water sits instead of draining, an unpolished weld that turns into a black line and then rust, or a shelf that flexes when fully loaded.

So we design around one idea: fast to clean and built to take punishment. Internal corners with a radius a cloth can reach, a slight fall on wet surfaces toward the drainage point, continuous polished welds instead of spot welds that leave gaps, and a thickness and leg design based on the real load rather than an average.

Dimensions are the biggest advantage of bespoke fabrication: commercial kitchens in Jeddah are rarely standard sizes. We start from the space you actually have — aisle width, window height, drain position — so the unit fits without gaps and without cutting on site.

Details we build into every kitchen unit

  • Hemmed edges that are safe to touch, so no one is cut during a fast service rush.
  • Cleanable internal corners instead of sharp ones where food residue collects.
  • Adjustable legs to level the unit on uneven flooring, with bracing that stops wobble.
  • Under-shelves with calculated load capacity, because the lower shelf always carries more than expected.

What we need to quote a kitchen fit-out

  • Space dimensions: available length, width and height, and the narrowest doorway the unit must pass.
  • The list of units needed: tables, shelving, sinks — and how many of each.
  • The use type: prep, wash, cooking, storage — each changes the spec.
  • The required date, especially if it is tied to an opening.
