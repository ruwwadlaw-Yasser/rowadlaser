What goes wrong in metal supply for projects

In contracting projects the problem is rarely the first part. It shows up in the third batch, when parts arrive two millimetres off the previous ones — or when delivery slips a week and the installation crew stalls on site — or when the price changes mid-project on the excuse of metal costs.

These are three management problems, not technical ones, and we address them with three clear commitments: one fixed design file per part used across every batch, a written delivery schedule tied to your project phases, and a per-piece price we hold for an agreed period stated in writing in the quote.

Most importantly, one party is accountable to you. When parts are spread across several workshops the results diverge and every delay becomes an argument about who is at fault. With us there is one reference for the whole parts list, and personal follow-up instead of being passed between numbers.

What we need to price a project

  • The parts list (BOQ) with shapes, quantities, metal type and thickness for each item.
  • The files or drawings — even preliminary ones; we review them and flag anything needing clarification before pricing.
  • The project schedule: when each group of parts is needed on site, so we can build the delivery plan around it.
  • Will the order repeat? Repeat orders are priced differently from a single large batch.
