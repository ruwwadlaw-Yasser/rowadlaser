Why bending sometimes fails — and how we prevent it

Bending is not just folding a sheet. When a sheet bends, the metal stretches on the outside and compresses on the inside, which means the flat blank length differs from the sum of the finished leg lengths. That difference is the bend allowance, and ignoring it is the number-one reason a part comes out a few millimetres short or long — then gets rejected at installation.

The second reason is the inside bend radius. Every metal and thickness has a minimum radius; go below it and the outer face cracks or shows a clear fracture line. Stainless and aluminium are more sensitive to this than plain steel, and the aluminium alloy itself matters: 5052 tolerates a tighter bend than 6061.

That is why we review your design before cutting, not just before bending: we check bend order and tool access, the grain direction on brushed metal, and the distance between a bend and the nearest hole — because a hole too close to the bend line will deform permanently.

What we verify on every part before bending

  • Bend order: can every bend be made without the part itself blocking the tool on the next step?
  • Bend allowance for each individual bend, multiplied across the count — because small errors accumulate.
  • Distance from each bend to any hole or edge, to avoid deformed holes or torn edges.
  • Grain direction on brushed stainless, so faces match visually after assembly.

How metals behave when bent

What changes from metal to metal

The same design can succeed in one metal and crack in another. This is what we account for before production.

MetalBending behaviourWhat we allow for
Mild steelThe most forgiving: it tolerates relatively tight bends without cracking.Slight springback after the load is released, compensated by adjusting the angle.
Stainless steelHarder and more resistant, with greater springback than steel.Higher springback compensation, and protecting the brushed surface from tool marks.
Aluminium 5052High formability — the best choice for multiple bends and hemmed edges.A suitable radius to avoid a fracture line on the outer face.
Aluminium 6061Mechanically stronger but less tolerant of tight bends.A larger radius, and a design review if the bends are tight.
Galvanised sheetBendable, though the coating can flake at very tight bends.A wider radius to keep the zinc coating intact.

Practical limits for length, thickness and angle vary by order — send the drawing with final dimensions and angles and we confirm feasibility before starting.

How we work

From drawing to formed part

Reading the final dimensions

We start from the finished size you actually need, then work back to calculate the flat blank.

Allowances and cutting

We add the bend allowance for each bend and cut the blank to the calculated flat size with hole positions.

Bending in a planned order

We bend in an order that keeps tool access to every bend, and check the angle on the first piece before completing the batch.

Measurement and delivery

We measure angles and lengths on the finished part and deliver it assembly-ready on the agreed date.

Send these details to price the bending

  • A drawing with the finished dimensions after bending, not the flat blank — send both if you have them.
  • The number of bends and their angles, plus the inside radius if your design specifies one.
  • Metal type and thickness — and for aluminium, the alloy if possible.
  • Does the brushed face need a specific visible direction? Will the part be welded or coated after bending?
