What is laser cutting, and why does it beat traditional cutting?

Laser cutting is a thermal process: a focused beam melts the metal and separates it along a path defined in software. That path comes from your own file — an engineering drawing or a DXF — so the part matches the drawing to within ±0.1 mm, and part number 500 is identical to part number 1.

The practical difference from shearing, plasma or sawing shows up in three places: the edge comes out clean and usually needs no grinding, the heat-affected zone is narrow so the sheet does not distort, and the design does not restrict you — fine holes, sharp corners and cut-out patterns are as easy as a straight line.

That means real savings in finishing time and in material, because part nesting is calculated digitally before cutting starts to keep scrap to a minimum — and that shows up directly in the price we quote you.

What every cutting order gives you

  • Parts built to the drawing with clean, smooth edges, ready for the next stage — bending, welding or assembly.
  • Exact repeatability across a batch: every piece is a copy of the one before it — essential for any assembly or production line.
  • Design freedom: cut-out patterns, stencilled lettering, bolt holes in mixed diameters, and narrow strips that traditional methods struggle with.
  • Volume flexibility: from a sample to test an idea, to repeat batches for an ongoing project — same accuracy, same follow-up.

And if you do not have a file ready, send a hand sketch or a photo of the old part with its main dimensions — we will help turn it into a cuttable file before production starts.

Metal selection guide

Which metal should you choose?

Each metal behaves differently under the laser and ages differently in service. Here is a quick summary to help you decide — and each metal has its own detailed page.

MetalHow it behaves under the laserBest suited for
Stainless steel 304 / 316A clean, bright edge that resists oxidation; needs the right assist gas for an oxide-free finish.Kitchens and restaurants, medical fittings, façades, and anything touching water or food.
Mild / carbon steelThe easiest and cheapest metal to cut, but it rusts unless painted or galvanised afterwards.Frames, bases, structural elements and parts that will be painted or hidden inside an assembly.
AluminiumLight and reflective; needs finer power and speed control to avoid dross on the underside.Signage, moving parts, and anywhere weight or corrosion resistance without coating matters.
Copper and brassHighly reflective, highly conductive metals cut on a fibre laser with dedicated settings.Premium décor, logos, plaques and conductive electrical parts.
Galvanised sheetThe zinc layer protects against rust but is affected by cutting heat, so cut placement and post-treatment are planned.HVAC ducting, roofing, and moisture-exposed parts on a tighter budget.

Available thicknesses vary by metal and by order — send us the thickness and size you need and we will confirm feasibility and price before starting.
