What separates stainless that lasts from stainless that rusts

Most rust complaints in stainless work are not about the metal — they are about how it was fabricated. Three common mistakes are enough to ruin a sound part: surface contamination by iron particles from tools used on plain steel, welds left with their heat-tint layer uncleaned, and deep scratches that break the natural protective layer and become a starting point for corrosion.

Stainless protects itself with an invisible chromium-oxide layer that regenerates in the presence of oxygen. The fabricator’s job is to leave that layer intact: dedicated stainless tooling, cleaning of weld zones, and polishing along the grain rather than across it. This is not a luxury — it is the difference between a part that stays and a part that gets replaced.

In Jeddah specifically, the salty coastal air multiplies the effect of any of these mistakes. That is why every stainless order starts with a question about where it will be installed and in what environment — before we ask about dimensions — because the answer sets the grade, the finish and the handling method.

What full fabrication covers with us

  • Laser cutting the sheets to the final unit dimensions with every required hole and cut-out.
  • Bending sides and edges at exact angles, with hemmed edges that are safe to touch where users reach.
  • Welding the joints, then cleaning and polishing them so they visually disappear and regain corrosion resistance.
  • Fitting legs, handles, castors and shelves to spec, then checking stability and level.

Fabrication decisions

Decisions that set the unit’s lifespan

Four decisions we take with you before production — each one shows its effect years later, not days.

DecisionOptionsOur recommendation
Grade304, 316 or 430 depending on environment and budget.316 for any unit near the sea or in contact with salt water or strong cleaners; 304 for interiors.
FinishBrushed, mirror or 2B.Brushed for daily-use surfaces since it hides scratches and fingerprints; mirror for decorative elements only.
Joining methodContinuous welding, spot welding or bolted assembly.Continuous, polished welds for food and wet surfaces, because they leave no crevices for residue.
ThicknessSet by the load and the span between supports.Heavier for surfaces that are loaded or cut on; lighter for non-structural decorative panels.

How we work

From dimensions to a finished unit

Measure and specify

We agree size, load and environment with you, and fix the right grade and finish before anything is made.

Cut and bend

Laser cutting the sheets, then bending sides and edges at exact angles ready for assembly.

Weld and assemble

Welding and cleaning the joints, fitting legs, shelves and handles, and verifying stability.

Polish and deliver

Polishing along the grain, checking dimensions and edges, and packing that protects the surface until installation.

What we need to quote a stainless unit

  • The unit’s three dimensions (length × width × height) — and if it fits an existing space, send that too.
  • Use and environment: kitchen, lab, outdoors, near the sea? This sets the grade.
  • Expected load on surfaces and shelves, and whether they will be cut or pounded on.
  • Required accessories: adjustable legs, castors, handles, under-shelves, drainage openings.
