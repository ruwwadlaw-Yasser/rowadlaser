Why a logo is engraved rather than printed

A printed promotional gift loses its logo after a few months of use — scratched, faded in the dishwasher, or removed by cleaning agents. The gift then turns from a lasting marketing tool into an anonymous object, and the budget spent on it is gone.

A laser-engraved logo is part of the metal. It survives years of daily use — which is exactly what you pay for in a promotional gift: that the person sees it every day and is reminded of you. Engraving also raises the perceived value of the product — an engraved piece looks more expensive than a printed one, even in the same material.

And if you manufacture a product you sell, an engraved logo does another job: it proves the product is genuinely yours, and makes it much harder to counterfeit or resell under another name.

Tips that make your logo come out best

  • A vector file (AI, SVG, PDF or DXF) gives the cleanest edges at any size — if unavailable, a high-resolution PNG is usually enough.
  • Logos with very fine detail need a larger marking area — we suggest simplifying small details when the space is tight.
  • A laser does not mark colours, it marks contrast. A colour logo is converted to greyscale or a single tone — we show you the preview first.
  • Curved surfaces (flasks and mugs) are possible, but the marking area on them is limited to a certain width — send a photo and we will confirm.
