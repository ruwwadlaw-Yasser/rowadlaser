Why a data plate is engraved and not printed

A data plate is not just an identification sticker — it is the only reference for an asset's model, serial number, capacity, voltage and year of manufacture. When that plate disappears, ordering a spare part, proving a warranty period or tracing a service record becomes expensive guesswork.

In a real factory environment — heat, oils, solvents, pressure washing and Jeddah's sun — print and stickers do not survive. Laser engraving changes the metal surface itself, so the plate stays readable for the equipment's entire life.

That is why many supply contracts and quality and compliance requirements specify that identification must be permanently marked rather than printed — particularly on industrial equipment, spare parts, pressure vessels and electrical gear.

What we usually put on a data plate

  • The manufacturer's or owner's name and logo, and the model or type designation.
  • Serial number, part number, lot or batch number, and year of manufacture.
  • Technical specifications: voltage, current, power, pressure, capacity, weight — depending on the equipment type.
  • A QR code or barcode linking the plate to a maintenance record or digital operating manual.
  • Compliance data, country of manufacture, and any symbol required by a regulator or the client's contract.

How we run serial numbering

A thousand plates with a thousand different numbers — no human error

You send the template and range

The fixed plate design plus the required number range (e.g. 0001 to 1000) or an Excel file of the numbers.

We program the sequence

The numbers are linked directly into the software so they advance automatically per piece with no manual step.

First-article sample for approval

We produce one plate and send a photo for your approval before running the full batch.

Production and verification

We run the batch, then verify the number sequence and scan-test the codes before packing and delivery.

What we need to price a plate order

  • The plate size in millimetres, or a photo of the old plate if you are replacing one.
  • The required content: fixed text, variable fields, and the logo if any.
  • The total count, and the numbering range if sequential.
  • Mounting method: screws (we add the holes), industrial adhesive, or spot welding.
