Metal choice: the decision that sets half the cost

The biggest waste we see in metal projects is not in production but in selection: a dry interior part made in 316 stainless doubles its cost for no benefit, while an outdoor part exposed to rain made in bare steel rusts within months and gets remade. Getting it right takes only two questions: where will the part work, and what load will it carry?

So we start from the use, not the metal. Send us the part’s description, function and environment, and we will recommend the metal, thickness and protection — and if the cheaper option is enough for your need, we will say so plainly rather than selling you a costlier metal to inflate the invoice.

Once the metal is chosen, the fabrication chain is the same for all of them: precise laser cutting, exact-angle bending, welding suited to the metal, then finishing and inspection before delivery. The differences are in the details — weld heat, bend radius and surface treatment — and those details decide whether the part lasts.

The metals we work with

  • Stainless steel in 304, 316 and 430 — for wet and food environments and a lasting appearance.
  • Mild and carbon steel — for frames and load-bearing parts, with coating or galvanising for protection.
  • Aluminium in its common alloys — when weight or coating-free corrosion resistance matters.
  • Copper and brass — for premium decorative work and conductive electrical applications.
  • Galvanised sheet and coated metals — for HVAC, ducting and moisture-exposed parts on a smaller budget.

  • A photo or drawing of the required part, or of the old part you want replaced.
  • A description of the environment: indoor, outdoor, wet, coastal, food-contact, high heat.
