Aluminium laser cutting

When weight or corrosion resistance without coating matters, aluminium is the answer: roughly a third of steel’s weight, and it does not rust. But it is reflective and thermally conductive, so it needs finer cutting control — which is our job.

Why aluminium needs a different cutting approach

Aluminium reflects a large share of the beam’s energy and conducts heat away quickly, so heat escapes the cut zone instead of concentrating in it. Cutting it therefore needs different power, speed and gas pressure than steel — otherwise dross sticks to the underside of the edge and needs hand cleaning.

With correct settings the edge comes out clean and sharp and the sheet stays flat with no thermal distortion. That matters most in signage and large covers, where any bow in the panel is obvious once installed.

And because aluminium scratches more easily than stainless, we keep the protective film on during handling, and flag it if your order will later be anodised or coated — since that changes how the surface must be handled.

Common alloys and where they fit

  • 5052: excellent corrosion resistance and high formability — the first choice for signage, covers and parts that will be bent.
  • 6061: mechanically stronger and better for load-bearing parts and mechanical assemblies, but less forgiving in tight bends.
  • 1050 / pure aluminium: best for heavy forming and decorative uses, and the least strong.
  • Not sure which alloy? Describe the part’s use and expected loads and we will recommend the right one at no extra cost.
